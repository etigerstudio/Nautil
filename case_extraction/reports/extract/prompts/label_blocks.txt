You are preparing an investigation report for a reasoning benchmark. The benchmark shows a model only the FACTUAL record and hides everything the investigators concluded from it.

Below are the blocks of one report in document order: an index, the heading or opening words, the length, and the first part of the body.

Label every block with exactly one role:
  factual        — what was observed, measured, recorded, tested or stated: the occurrence narrative, the site examination, technical and laboratory examinations and their measured outcomes, witness accounts, weather, maintenance, design and complaint records, data recordings, counts and tables.
  mixed          — a block that states observed fact AND the investigators' reasoning about it in the same passage. Many boards write their whole technical account this way, under a heading such as "Key facts and analysis", "Investigation analysis" or "Discussion": measurements, test results and witness accounts sit next to the argument drawn from them. Prefer this label over `analysis` whenever the block clearly carries facts a reader would need.
  analysis       — the investigators' reasoning and almost nothing else: weighing explanations, ruling possibilities in or out, attributing cause, assessing whether a defect trend exists.
  conclusion     — stated findings, causes, contributory factors, the summary of conclusions, or the decision that closes the investigation.
  recommendation — safety recommendations, actions taken, learning points, manufacturer actions.
  front_matter   — title page, legal notice, contents, glossary, preface, foreword, distribution list, page furniture.
  appendix       — appendices, annexes, references, abbreviations, index of figures or cited complaint numbers.

Rules:
- A block that mixes observed fact with reasoning is `mixed`, not `analysis`. Only a block that is essentially pure argument is `analysis`.
- When in doubt between `factual` and `mixed`, choose `mixed`: the sentences that give the answer away are removed later, one by one.
- A summary that says what happened AND why is `conclusion`.
- A chronology of what the investigation did and received, with no assessment, is `factual`.
