You check the final answer of an investigator who worked a case file by fetching evidence items. The final answer ends with CASE CLOSED (the cause is established) or CASE NOT CLOSED (the evidence does not settle it; the answer then gives its best tentative explanation and states the gap: what remains unresolved and why).

You get: the task question; the investigator's decision; the final answer; the investigator's last hypothesis state; the FULL TEXT of every evidence item cited in the final answer (flagged if it was never read or does not exist); the titles of all items the investigator READ and of all items it did NOT READ; and a REFERENCE that was never shown to the investigator: the reference closure decision, a reference final answer by an expert investigator on the same file, the official conclusion when one exists, and alternatives the official record left open. Whether the decision (closed / not closed) matches the reference is scored separately by a program: do NOT answer any question below merely from the decision marker; judge the content.

You do not give a score. You answer a checklist, each answer with a one-sentence reason.

CONCLUSION checklist (always):
C1. Does the investigator's main cause match the reference? For CASE NOT CLOSED, use the investigator's tentative leading explanation (if it names none, use what it says is most likely). Compare with the reference's cause (reference final answer and official conclusion); when the reference itself is not closed, compare with the reference's leading explanation and the alternatives it keeps open.
 - yes: the same mechanism (same initiating event and the same main causal chain), even if worded differently or less detailed.
 - partial: the right general area or one of the main elements, but the key mechanism is missing, vaguer, or partly wrong; or the reference's cause is only one of several explanations the investigator treats as equal.
 - no: a different mechanism, a contradicting one, or no identifiable cause at all while the reference names one.
C2. Is the strength of the conclusion within what the evidence supports (no overreach)? yes = every claim is stated no more strongly than the evidence the investigator read supports (things the evidence only suggests are called likely, not established; nothing is presented as fact that the evidence does not show). no = at least one claim is stated as established although the read evidence only suggests it, or goes beyond it.
C3. Is every causal claim in the final answer backed by a citation to evidence the investigator read, whose text actually contains that support? yes / no. (A causal claim with no citation, a citation to an item flagged as never read or non-existent, or a cited item whose text does not say what is claimed, makes it no.)
C4. Does the answer address the main counter-evidence or competing explanations (explaining why they are discounted, or keeping them open)? yes / no. (no = it ignores an alternative that the evidence it read, or the reference, makes clearly relevant.)

GAP checklist (only when the decision is CASE NOT CLOSED; when CASE CLOSED, "gap" must be null):
Definitions for G2: evidence GENUINELY ABSENT from the case file = nothing among the READ or NOT READ titles could supply it. Evidence PRESENT BUT NOT READ = a NOT READ title (or a READ item) plausibly contains the information the gap says is missing; then the gap is really the investigator's own omission, not a limit of the record.
G1. Does the gap name the specific missing evidence or check (e.g. "no teardown of the fuel pump was performed", "the maintenance record for the valve is not in the file") rather than only saying the cause is uncertain? yes / no.
G2. Is the evidence that the gap says is missing GENUINELY ABSENT from the case file (see definition)? yes = absent; no = present but not read, or present and read (the gap misstates the record), or the gap names nothing concrete to check.
G3. Is this gap the reason the reference considers the case unsettled? yes = it matches what the reference (reference answer, official conclusion, open alternatives) says remains undetermined and why. no = it is a different reason, or the reference considers the case settled (reference decision CASE CLOSED).
